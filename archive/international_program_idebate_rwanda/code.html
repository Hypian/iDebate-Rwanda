The US Tour: Voices from a Renewed Nation

At the occasion of the 20th commemoration of the Genocide against the Tutsi, a group of 6 young Rwandans took on the task of sharing their stories with the world — the pain, confusion and frustrations of being born into a society ravaged by genocide, alongside their hope in a renewed Rwanda and the power of "engaging the other."

FOUNDATIONAL MANDATE

The Three Pillars of the Diplomatic Tour

Each university residency is structured around deliberate moral engagement, historical accountability, and intellectual interrogation.

01
AWARENESS & HISTORICAL ACCOUNTABILITY

To Raise Awareness About Genocide

For decades, historical catastrophes of mass violence have been relegated to sterile textbooks, analyzed through distant academic theories that strip away the raw human tragedy. The iDebate US Tour alters this pedagogical distance entirely.

American university undergraduates and faculty members are invited into intimate, honest forums where Rwandan youth articulate the lived generational consequences of the 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi. Rather than abstract statistics, our debaters share the precise emotional architecture of reconstruction: growing up in neighborhoods where survivors and perpetrators live alongside each other, negotiating forgiveness, and reconstructing an shattered social fabric from ground zero.

02
THE PEDAGOGY OF CIVILITY

Dialogue as Antidote to Polarization

In an era where Western democratic institutions wrestle with acute political tribalism and dehumanizing rhetoric, Rwandan youth offer a powerful perspective born of survival: that uncivil words, left unchecked, build the moral runway for violence.

DEMOCRATIC ETHICS & RHETORIC

To Raise Awareness About the Importance of Civil Discourse

Young Rwandans who came of age in the wake of societal disintegration recognize that debate is never merely an academic exercise; it is an existential safeguard. When disagreements turn toxic and opponents are framed as mortal enemies, the social contract fractures.

By modeling rigorous, respectful, and fact-grounded debate on contentious international and domestic motions, the delegation demonstrates to global audiences how dialogue transforms polarization into durable peace. They challenge students to separate ideas from identities, listening not to refute, but to comprehend.

03
GLOBAL SOLIDARITY & FELLOWSHIP

To Create a Cross-Cultural Experience

For decades, the global portrayal of the African continent has been trapped within a monolithic narrative of crisis, pity, and perpetual need. The US Tour serves as an intentional disruption of these outdated tropes.

Through reciprocal intellectual exchange, American collegiate scholars encounter Rwandan peers who are sharp philosophers, poised litigators, and innovative civic leaders. This authentic interaction dissolves preconceptions on both sides, replacing superficial stereotypes with mutual empathy, shared laughter, joint academic research, and friendships that endure for decades.
